How to work out the landed cost of a generator or transformer from China

A supplier's FOB price is only the starting point. To compare offers and set your budget, work out the cost delivered to your site.

The building blocks

  • FOB price: equipment loaded on the ship in China (Shanghai, Qingdao or Ningbo).
  • Ocean freight: depends on container type and route. A 1,000 kW open-frame set usually takes one 40 ft high-cube container; small silent sets can share a container.
  • Insurance: typically about 0.3% of the cargo and freight value.
  • CIF = FOB + freight + insurance. This is what our instant estimate shows for generator sets.
  • Destination port charges and customs clearance: terminal handling, documentation and the broker's fee.
  • Import duty and VAT: set by your country's tariff for the HS code. Generator sets usually fall under HS 8502 and transformers under HS 8504; rates vary widely, and some projects are exempt.
  • Inland transport and offloading: often underestimated for heavy units; a crane may be needed on site.

A quick way to budget

  1. Get the CIF figure from your supplier or our estimate.
  2. Ask your clearing agent for the duty and VAT rate for the HS code and the usual port charges.
  3. Add inland transport and offloading.
  4. Keep 5–10% contingency for exchange rates and demurrage.

Watch for

  • Free time at the destination port: delays in clearance quickly cost money.
  • Pre-shipment inspection or conformity certificates some countries require before the goods leave China.
  • Fuel: generator sets ship with little or no fuel; budget the first fill.
